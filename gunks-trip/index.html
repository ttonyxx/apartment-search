The shape of it

One road does all the work.

Every problem on this list sits along the Undercliff carriage road in the Trapps, north of the Trapps Bridge. That is the whole trick to a car-free Gunks day: you get dropped at one trailhead, walk a flat gravel road, and the boulders come to you in grade order. Peterskill is better rock for some of this, but it is a further eight miles with a 30-pass daily cap — wrong call on a bus day.

Climbing window ≈ 7 hours Boots on around 09:35, last burn at 17:45. The bus, not the light, is what ends the day.
Ground covered ≈ 2.5 miles Flat carriage road, Trapps Bridge to the Andrew Area and back. Bring shoes you can walk in.
On the list 30 problems Two warm-ups and then V3 through V9, four stations, all of it inside Mohonk Preserve on one day pass.

Saturday, minute by minute

The clock.

Two things on this schedule are immovable and everything else bends around them: the 20:00 bus, and Rock and Snow's 19:00 close — which is your crash-pad return deadline, since they don't take pads in the after-hours drop box. Work backwards from those.

Walking north from the bridge

Four stations.

Walk times are from the Trapps Bridge, where the carriage road crosses above Route 44/55. The order below is both the map and the plan — soft grades and cold fingers at the start, the hard stuff at the far end when you're warm, then back to Steel Bridge for a last cool-of-the-day burn on the way out.

Station 010 min

Steel Bridge Boulders

09:35 → 10:15  ·  and again 17:00 → 17:45

Twenty-odd problems scattered within a stone's throw of the bridge itself. The obvious warm-up zone, and — because you'll pass it again on the walk out — the right place to spend your last, coolest, best-friction hour.

Save for the evening: New Pair of Glasses (V7) and Jackson Pollock (V8).

Station 023 min

Keyhole Cliff

10:15 → 11:30

The first cliff band directly abutting the road. Dense with four-star moderates and carrying the John Gill history — the Gill Egg and Gill Crack are the reason people still walk here first.

Don't miss: The Gill Egg (V4) — dynamic, four stars, everyone's favourite Gunks V4.

Station 035 min

Pebbles Boulders

11:30 → 13:30

200 feet past the Uberfall outhouse. Three big boulders that came to rest on their sides, so all the Gunks horizontals are now verticals. The best density of classics on the road.

The one to send: Black Boulder Problem (V5), 4.5 stars — the marquee moderate of the Trapps.

Station 04≈ 20 min

Andrew Area

14:00 → 16:30

The far end, and the hardest. An obvious roof with a crowd under it. This is where the V7–V9 tick list lives, and why the plan puts you here at peak warmth rather than first thing.

The day's objective: The Buddha (V7−), then Dragon Turns (V9) if there's skin left.

84°F and no wind

Climbing in the wrong conditions, well.

Be honest about this: a sunny 84°F July day is poor Gunks bouldering weather. Quartzite conglomerate needs cold to feel sticky, and the crimps on Buddha will feel like soap by one o'clock. The plan is built around that rather than pretending otherwise.

Where the shade is

  • The Undercliff corridor is mixed oak-hemlock forest — nearly all of these boulders sit under canopy rather than in the open, so you're fighting humidity, not sun.
  • The cliff band faces broadly south-east: morning light hits the wall, afternoon puts the road into cliff shadow. Later is cooler here, which is why the evening Steel Bridge session exists.
  • Andrew's roof problems are genuinely overhung and shaded all day. If anything is going down at 2pm in July, it's roof holds.

Tactics for greasy rock

  • Front-load the crimps. Anything face-and-crimp — Gill Egg, Black Boulder, Cronin Face — wants the 10am–1pm window.
  • Two burns, then move on. On a hot day the third attempt is worse than the second. Rest 5+ minutes between goes or don't bother.
  • Brush every hold, every go. A boar's-hair brush is the single highest-value item in your pack tomorrow.
  • Chalk is not the answer, drying is. Liquid chalk base layer in the morning; a towel on the pad for your hands between attempts.
  • 3+ litres of water each. There is no water on the carriage road. Fill up in New Paltz.

Getting there without a car

The three things that can sink this.

1 · Book the return ride before you lose signal

Cell reception at the West Trapps lot and along the back of the cliffs is unreliable to nonexistent. Standing in the parking lot at 18:00 trying to summon a Lyft is the classic way to miss the bus. Schedule the 18:15 pickup while you still have bars in town, or get the driver's number on the way up and text them to come back.

2 · The crash pad question

Rock and Snow at 44 Main St rents pads and opens 08:00 Saturday — three minutes' walk from the bus stop, so the timing works. But they close at 19:00 and won't accept pads in the drop box, which makes the return the real deadline of your day. If you'd rather not be governed by that, bring your own pads on the bus; Trailways will take them in the hold.

3 · The Mohonk day pass

Climbing anywhere in the Trapps needs a Mohonk Preserve day pass — $20 per person as of last season. Buy it at Rock and Snow with the pads, or from the rangers who staff the steel bridge and the West Trapps lot on weekends. Keep the wristband: the $20 credits toward an annual pass if you come back.

Ride, in short

New Paltz bus stop → West Trapps Trailhead is about 15 minutes and $20–30 by Uber, Lyft or local taxi. From the lot, walk up the ramp to the Trapps Bridge in five to eight minutes — that's where the carriage road and this whole list begins.

Pack

  • Boar's-hair brush ×2
  • Liquid chalk + loose chalk
  • Tape, nail clippers
  • 3L water each
  • Approach shoes — 2.5 mi of gravel

Food

  • Buy in New Paltz — nothing on the road

  • Electrolytes, not just water
  • Something salty for the 15:00 dip

Know before

  • No potable water on Undercliff
  • Outhouse at the Uberfall
  • Patchy fog until 08:00 — rock may be damp early
  • Signal dies past the bridge

Hard deadlines

  • 19:00 — pads back to Rock and Snow
  • 20:00 — bus departs New Paltz
  • Leave the Andrew Area by 16:30

If it goes sideways

  • Rock is wet at 09:30. Morning fog is forecast to burn off by 08:00, but conglomerate holds damp. Start at Keyhole rather than Steel Bridge — it's the cliff band, and it dries first up off the ground.
  • Too hot to try hard by noon. Drop the Andrew push, spend the afternoon volume-climbing four-star V3s and V4s at Keyhole and Pebbles, and go all-in on the 17:00 Steel Bridge session.
  • Skin's gone by 15:00. That's a normal July outcome. Walk to Andrew anyway and try Buddha twice — knowing the moves is worth the trip back in October.
  • Running late for the bus. Leave the Andrew Area no later than 16:30. If you're still out at 18:00, skip the evening Steel Bridge session, not the pad return.
