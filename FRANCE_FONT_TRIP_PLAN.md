## Weather and sandstone rules

Mountain Project’s area climate table gives October averages around **16°C/61°F high and 8°C/46°F low**, with roughly seven precipitation days. Pack for cold mornings, mild afternoons, and rain.

The non-negotiable decision rule:

1. If the boulder is damp, dark with moisture, cool/wet under an overhang, or the top-out is wet, do not climb it.
2. Do not try to “dry” holds with chalk, towels, or fans and then climb. Water remains inside the porous sandstone and weakens it.
3. After rain, inspect several aspects and the top—not just one sunny face. If uncertain, use Karma, the château, Barbizon, Moret-sur-Loing, or the spa.

Local ethics also require:

- Clean sand from shoes on a carpet square before every attempt.
- Use a soft brush, chalk sparingly, and brush away tick marks/chalk before leaving.
- Carry pads rather than dragging them; stay on existing paths.
- No fires, forest sleeping, or night climbing. Forest car parks are closed 10:00 PM–6:00 AM.
- Do not climb or brush engraved/petroglyph boulders.

See [Bleau.info ethics](https://bleau.info/ethique?locale=en) for the local standard.

## Gear and daily carry

- Two large/triple pads minimum; three or a larger group for high/complex landings.
- Two pairs of shoes each if available: one comfortable circuit pair, one precise project pair.
- Clean carpet square/doormat, soft natural/synthetic climbing brush, modest chalk, tape, nail clippers/file, and skin balm for after climbing.
- Warm layers, waterproof shell, hat, spare socks, and dry travel shoes.
- 1.5–2 L water per person, thermos, lunch, and trash bag.
- Small tarp only to keep bags/pads dry—never use it to justify climbing wet rock.
- Headlamp for walking out only, not night climbing.
- Offline maps plus a Font-specific app/guide. Mountain Project alone is not comprehensive enough for navigation.
- Basic first aid and travel insurance that covers outdoor bouldering.
