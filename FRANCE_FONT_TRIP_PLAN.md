# Tony + Katherine: Fontainebleau climbing trip

**Dates:** Saturday, October 17–Sunday, October 25, 2026<br>
**Base:** Fontainebleau/Avon<br>
**Trip shape:** five outdoor climbing days, two recovery/culture days, and a low-stress airport night<br>
**Special dates:** Katherine’s birthday Sunday, October 18; Tony’s birthday Thursday, October 22

## Assumptions to confirm

- The return flight on October 25 is a morning departure from **Paris Charles de Gaulle (CDG)**. If it is Orly, the final-night logistics need to change.
- You are staying in or close to Fontainebleau/Avon and will have a rental car. A car makes this plan substantially easier because the climbing sectors are spread around the forest.
- Your climbing grades were not provided. Every climbing day therefore has an easier circuit lane, a middle classics lane, and a harder project lane.
- No restaurant, spa, car, pad, château, or hotel reservation has been made by this plan.

## Day-by-day plan

### Saturday, October 17 — London to Fontainebleau

- **5:00 AM:** arrive at London St Pancras. Your screenshot shows Eurostar departing at **6:31 AM** and arriving Paris Gare du Nord at **9:57 AM**. Eurostar’s published guidance is to allow 60–90 minutes before a London departure.
- **10:30–11:30 AM:** pick up a prebooked compact estate/small SUV near Gare du Nord. Book an automatic if needed and confirm a **one-way CDG return on October 24**. Two folded large pads plus luggage require more space than a tiny city car.
- **11:30 AM–1:30 PM:** drive to Fontainebleau, allowing for Paris traffic; stop for lunch if needed.
- **2:00–3:00 PM:** check in, then collect two large/triple crash pads. [Climb Fontainebleau](https://www.climb-fontainebleau.com/location-crash-pad) in Arbonne currently lists locker pickup/return and triple pads at €90 per week each. Reserve ahead and follow its cash/payment instructions. Add a third pad or join other climbers before trying high or poor-landed problems.
- **3:30–5:00 PM:** groceries, bakery supplies, water, tape, and a cheap clean doormat/carpet square for shoes.
- **5:00 PM onward:** short château-garden or town walk, early dinner, and sleep. Do not squeeze in a tired first-day bouldering session.

### Sunday, October 18 — Katherine’s birthday at Éléphant

This is designed as a beautiful, celebratory first climbing day rather than a max-effort day.

- **8:00 AM:** Fontainebleau market at Place de la République. Assemble a birthday picnic: pastries, fruit, cheese, bread, something sparkling for after climbing, and a small cake. The official tourism office lists the market Sunday morning from 8:00 AM–1:00 PM.
- **9:30 AM:** drive to **Éléphant**. Warm up slowly on the painted circuits and keep enough energy for dinner.
- **10:15 AM–3:30 PM:** climb from the Éléphant menu below. Prioritize variety—slab, mantle, arete, roof—over volume. Stop while everyone still has skin.
- **4:00–6:30 PM:** return, shower, nap, and dress up.
- **7:15 PM:** reserve **L’Axel**, 43 rue de France. It has one Michelin star, serves modern French food with Japanese influence, and currently takes Sunday dinner bookings from 7:15–9:00 PM. Tell the restaurant that it is Katherine’s birthday when reserving.

**If it rains:** keep the market and L’Axel. Replace climbing with the Château de Fontainebleau (open Sunday 9:30 AM–5:00 PM in October) and, if desired, a prebooked spa treatment.

### Monday, October 19 — Bas Cuvier classics

- **8:00 AM:** leave early with breakfast and lunch. Bas Cuvier is iconic and popular.
- **8:30–9:15 AM:** long circuit-based warm-up. Font top-outs, polished feet, and technical slabs can feel much harder than the conversion grade suggests.
- **9:15 AM–3:30 PM:** choose one lane from the Bas Cuvier menu, then add at most one true project. Cuvier Est is a short second stop only if energy and skin are good.
- **4:00 PM onward:** food, shower, finger/skin care, and a genuinely easy evening.

### Tuesday, October 20 — recovery, town, and spa

- Sleep in. The Fontainebleau market also runs Tuesday morning from 8:00 AM–1:00 PM.
- Late morning/early afternoon: wander **Moret-sur-Loing** and have a long lunch, or simply stay in town with no timetable.
- **3:30–5:30 PM:** reserve a couples recovery treatment at [Hôtel & Spa Napoléon](https://hotelnapoleon-fontainebleau.com/fr/page/hotel-spa-fontainebleau.17787.html). It accepts outside guests for treatments, is open daily, and includes one hour of pool/sauna/hammam access before a treatment of at least an hour, subject to reservation.
- No hard indoor climbing. If weather has already forced two complete outdoor rest days, [Karma Fontainebleau](https://karma-escalade.fr/en/fontainebleau/the-gym) is the rain fallback; weekday hours are currently noon–10:00 PM.

The Château de Fontainebleau is closed Tuesday, which is why it is not the recovery-day anchor.

### Wednesday, October 21 — Franchard Isatis and Cuisinière

- **8:30 AM:** depart for Franchard.
- **9:00–10:00 AM:** warm up on the Isatis blue/red circuit problems, keeping attempts low.
- **10:00 AM–3:30 PM:** work through the Isatis menu. Move to Cuisinière only if specifically trying Beatle Juice or if the first zone is crowded.
- **3:30 PM:** mandatory stop unless fingertips and elbows are unusually fresh. Tomorrow is Tony’s birthday climbing day.
- Casual dinner in Fontainebleau and prepare the birthday picnic.

### Thursday, October 22 — Tony’s birthday at Roche aux Sabots + Cul de Chien

- Birthday breakfast at the lodging or a patisserie.
- **9:00 AM:** arrive at the large Roche aux Sabots/Cul de Chien parking area.
- **9:15 AM–12:30 PM:** warm up and climb the blue/red circuits at **La Roche aux Sabots**. Mountain Project specifically recommends this as an excellent first-Font area with several circuits and a very short approach.
- **12:30 PM:** birthday picnic.
- **1:15–3:30 PM:** walk through the sand to **Le Cul de Chien**. Try the famous roof only with sufficient pads and competent spotting; otherwise make the sand sea and dog-head boulder the destination and finish on Roche aux Sabots classics.
- **3:30 PM:** toast only after the climbing is over; return for shower and rest.
- **7:00 PM:** reserve [Le Magnum](https://www.lemagnum-fontainebleau.fr/en/) for cocktails, charcoal-grilled meat/shared plates, and a relaxed birthday dinner. It is currently open Thursday 6:00–11:30 PM and explicitly handles birthday events. Ask for a candle/dessert when booking. If that menu is not your style, substitute Gina at Hôtel de Cavoye, which is open daily and has a cocktail bar.

**If it rains:** keep the dinner. Use the Château de Fontainebleau, a couples spa treatment, or Karma instead of touching damp sandstone.

### Friday, October 23 — château and Barbizon rest day

- **8:00–9:00 AM:** optional Friday market breakfast/picnic shopping.
- **9:30 AM–12:00 PM:** [Château de Fontainebleau](https://www.fontainebleau-tourisme.com/en/fiche/670011/chateau-de-fontainebleau/). October hours are 9:30 AM–5:00 PM, last entry 4:15 PM; the tourism office recommends about two hours and arrival before 3:00 PM.
- Lunch in town.
- **2:00–5:00 PM:** Barbizon village and the Barbizon School museum. The museum is open Friday in October from 10:00 AM–12:30 PM and 2:00–6:00 PM.
- Early dinner, pack everything except Saturday’s climbing kit, and confirm the pad-return and car-return instructions.

### Saturday, October 24 — victory lap and CDG transition

- **8:00–11:30 AM:** choose the closest fully dry sector and do a low-risk victory lap. Revisit favorite unfinished moves, complete an easier circuit, and avoid exhausted highball attempts.
- **Noon–1:00 PM:** lunch and return crash pads via the prearranged method.
- **1:00–2:00 PM:** shower/change at the lodging if checkout allows; otherwise use packed travel clothes and wipes.
- **By 2:00 PM:** depart for CDG. Traffic can expand the drive considerably, so do not make a late-afternoon project plan.
- Return the rental car, eat near the terminal, and sleep at a hotel serving your departure terminal. This protects the October 25 morning flight from Paris traffic and rental-return delays.

If the flight is actually from Orly, use an Orly hotel and return the car there instead.

### Sunday, October 25 — fly to NYC

- **Important clock change:** France ends daylight-saving time overnight on October 24–25, 2026; at 3:00 AM the clock returns to 2:00 AM. Network-connected phones should adjust automatically, but use a hotel wake-up call as backup and treat the ticket time as local post-change time.
- Follow the airline’s international check-in guidance and the exact terminal instructions.
- Carry climbing shoes in hand luggage if they are difficult to replace; pads stay in France.

## Weather and sandstone rules

Mountain Project’s area climate table gives October averages around **16°C/61°F high and 8°C/46°F low**, with roughly seven precipitation days. Pack for cold mornings, mild afternoons, and rain.

The non-negotiable decision rule:

1. If the boulder is damp, dark with moisture, cool/wet under an overhang, or the top-out is wet, do not climb it.
2. Do not try to “dry” holds with chalk, towels, or fans and then climb. Water remains inside the porous sandstone and weakens it.
3. After rain, inspect several aspects and the top—not just one sunny face. If uncertain, use Karma, the château, Barbizon, Moret-sur-Loing, or the spa.

Local ethics also require:

- Clean sand from shoes on a carpet square before every attempt.
- Use a soft brush, chalk sparingly, and brush away tick marks/chalk before leaving.
- Carry pads rather than dragging them; stay on existing paths.
- No fires, forest sleeping, or night climbing. Forest car parks are closed 10:00 PM–6:00 AM.
- Do not climb or brush engraved/petroglyph boulders.

See [Bleau.info ethics](https://bleau.info/ethique?locale=en) for the local standard.

## Gear and daily carry

- Two large/triple pads minimum; three or a larger group for high/complex landings.
- Two pairs of shoes each if available: one comfortable circuit pair, one precise project pair.
- Clean carpet square/doormat, soft natural/synthetic climbing brush, modest chalk, tape, nail clippers/file, and skin balm for after climbing.
- Warm layers, waterproof shell, hat, spare socks, and dry travel shoes.
- 1.5–2 L water per person, thermos, lunch, and trash bag.
- Small tarp only to keep bags/pads dry—never use it to justify climbing wet rock.
- Headlamp for walking out only, not night climbing.
- Offline maps plus a Font-specific app/guide. Mountain Project alone is not comprehensive enough for navigation.
- Basic first aid and travel insurance that covers outdoor bouldering.
